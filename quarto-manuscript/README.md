abstract: |
  The drastic economic disparities in Washington, District of Columbia often obscure the impacts of poverty and inequity upon residents living in working-class and low-income communities. The immense wealth in parts of the city skew citywide data and mask these impacts. This limits the capability of city officials to use data to inform education policy.
  Many data-driven projects do not examine data with such granularity as the school, census tract, block, or neighborhood level. Projects such as the Urban Institute’s Upward Mobility Dashboard help policymakers understand conditions affecting upward mobility. However, many of these projects only cover data at the county level, not the census tract level.
  Our project seeks to visualize and model the impacts of key educational conditions and levers on upward mobility and educational attainment outcomes. We use data pulled from the school, block, tract, neighborhood, and ward levels to model this for the Department of the Deputy Major of Education for the District of Colombia.
